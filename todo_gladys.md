## Current scope
- The model is a fixed-fleet 2023 snapshot with four nodes: Namibia (NAM) and its neighbours South Africa (ZAF), Zambia (ZMB) and Zimbabwe (ZWE); Zimbabwe connects only through Zambia (Kariba link, 428 MW). Historical annual trade volumes are stored for reference but are not enforced as dispatch limits.
- Fuel and electricity trade prices are included. Van Eck's variable and fixed O&M and Anixas I's fixed and variable O&M are taken from the NIRP 2022 report, whose values are based on 2020–2021 data; they are scaled from 2020 to 2023 prices with the Bank of Namibia CPI (167.3/140.6 = 1.1899). Anixas I uses an estimated 2023 delivered HFO price (N$306.5/GJ): the 2023 NW Europe FOB market price plus the NIRP 2020 delivery cost scaled with CPI, using the Bank of Namibia 2020 average rate of 18.7695 N$/EUR. Its NIRP variable O&M ("3 x N$1.55/kWh") is applied as N$4.65/kWh (N$4,650/MWh). South Africa (SAPP market, Eskom) trades at the 2023 monthly SAPP day-ahead price (€38–192/MWh); Zambia and Zimbabwe (ZESCO, ZPC) at NamPower's average import price (€95/MWh); exports earn the SAPP price capped at €95/MWh so contract imports are not resold. Electricity is traded only at the neighbour nodes and reaches Namibia over the power_line interconnectors, limited per direction to the SAPP transfer limits (575 MW South Africa, 180 MW Zambia; export limits assumed equal). Annual trade and fuel imports are unbounded; the yearly export limit is set explicitly to unbounded, because zen-creator's default of 0 blocks all exports. The interconnector limits do not bind in 2023: imports peak at 573 MW.
- Manual dispatch assumptions have been removed; dispatch is cost-driven. Hydro, PV and wind O&M are not yet included.
- With the monthly SAPP price, Van Eck (about €151/MWh) runs 38.1 GWh, all in November, when SAPP costs €192/MWh and the Zambia line is full; NamPower reported 44.3 GWh (FY2023/24). Anixas I (about €400/MWh) is never cheaper than imports and runs 0 GWh, against 5-6 GWh reported (FY2022/23 and FY2023/24); monthly prices are too smooth to capture its short emergency runs.
- Because SAPP is cheaper than the contract price in most months, the model buys 1,809 GWh via South Africa and 388 GWh via Zambia (Sep–Nov only), while NamPower actually bought about 676 GWh from Eskom and SAPP and 1,808 GWh from ZESCO and ZPC. Contract volumes (take-or-pay) are not modeled.
- Existing renewable capacity is limited to the 2023 roster. The separate future-expansion list is not included in the active fleet.
